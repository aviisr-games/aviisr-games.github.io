Baylight tips: how to clear a dock

The rules in our own words, a few habits that keep a dock from jamming, and the first moves of both browser docks.

The rules in short

A dock is a row of bays. Parcels arrive on a conveyor belt in a fixed order, and only the front parcel can be played. Each parcel has a colour, shown as a letter and a small symbol so that colour is never the only clue. You place the front parcel in a bay that is either empty or already holds that same colour. A bay holds three parcels at most.

The moment a bay reaches three parcels of one colour, that truck ships and the bay is empty again. The dock is cleared when the belt is finished and every bay and the pallet are empty.

Two extra mechanics appear on the browser docks. A locked lane is a bay you cannot use at first; it opens after your first shipped truck. The pallet is a parking spot for one parcel. When no bay will take the front parcel, you can park it there. As soon as a bay of the same colour has room, whether you just started it or just freed it, the parked parcel moves in by itself.

The belt order never changes, so a dock is a puzzle of planning, not of reflexes. There is no timer in the browser version, and you can undo as often as you like.

Eight habits that help

1. Read the belt ahead

The browser dock shows the front parcel and the next few behind it. Before you place the front parcel, look at what follows. If two more of its colour are coming soon, a half-filled bay of that colour is a good home. If the colour will not return for a long time, you may be about to block a bay for a long stretch.

2. Count colours against bays

Every colour that is partly loaded holds a bay until its third parcel arrives. If more colours are open at once than you have bays, you are stuck. So count how many colours are in flight, not only how full each bay is. The whole puzzle is about keeping that number at or below your bay count.

3. Keep one bay free when you can

A new colour needs somewhere to start. An empty bay is your only answer to a colour you have not seen yet, so spending the last empty bay on a parcel that could wait is a risky trade. Prefer a matching bay over an empty one when both are reasonable.

4. Finish sets of three

A bay that ships becomes empty at once. Finishing a nearly full bay is usually worth more than starting a new one, because it turns one occupied bay into one free bay. If a colour has two parcels in a bay and the third is on the belt now, take it.

5. Use the pallet for a lone parcel

The pallet holds one parcel, so it is best for a colour you have just one of, while the bays are busy with colours that are closer to finishing. It is a poor choice for a colour with two parcels waiting. Remember it is a single slot: once it is full, it cannot rescue you again until the parcel moves out.

6. Park early, not late

The pallet works best before the board is crowded. Parking a parcel while a bay is still free keeps your options open. Parking when every bay is full and the belt is about to deliver a fourth colour is often too late, because the parked parcel cannot move out until a bay of its colour has room.

7. Plan around the locked lane

On a dock with a locked lane you start with fewer bays than the dock will eventually have. Aim your first shipped truck at the earliest finish you can see. The sooner a set of three completes, the sooner the extra bay appears. Until then, avoid opening a third colour.

8. Use undo to test an idea

Undo costs nothing, so try the risky placement and look at the next three parcels. If the dock becomes a dead end, step back. If you want a hint instead, the Step Solver Move button plays one correct move from wherever you are, and says so plainly when the current position cannot be won.

Walkthrough: the first moves of Dock 5

Dock 5 has three bays, with one locked. The belt reads B Y B B R Y G R G Y R G (blue, yellow, red, green). Only two bays are open at the start.

  • Blue goes into bay 1 and yellow into bay 2. Both open bays are now in use.
  • The next two parcels are both blue, so bay 1 takes them. The third blue ships the truck. That empties bay 1 and opens the locked bay 3.
  • Red starts in bay 1, yellow joins its colour in bay 2, and green starts in bay 3.
  • Red and green follow into their own bays, then the last yellow, red and green each complete a set. Four trucks ship in twelve moves.

The lesson is the one from habit 7: with only two bays open, you must let a colour finish before a third can start. Here the belt delivers three blues in a row, so the lock opens early.

Walkthrough: why Dock 6 uses the pallet

Dock 6 has four bays, no locked lane and one pallet slot. The belt reads Y Y R R G B B P P B Y G P R G. The sequence we use for replay starts with both yellows in bay 1 and both reds in bay 2. Then green arrives, and the pallet comes in.

The reason is a count. By the time the purples arrive, yellow, red, green, blue and purple are all in flight. That is five colours and four bays. The green parcel is the one colour with a single parcel so far, so it is the cheapest to park. Blue takes bay 3 and purple takes bay 4. The third blue ships, the third yellow ships, and the next green lands in the freed bay 1, which pulls the parked green in with it. Purple, red and green then finish, five trucks in fifteen moves.
