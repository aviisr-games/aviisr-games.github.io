Naked Triples in Sudoku: Three-Cell Candidate Lock Guide

By Avi Israeli · Reviewed by Ninely Engine Logic Council · Updated November 2026

What is a Naked Triple?

A Naked Triple is the three-cell big brother of the Naked Pair. It appears when three cells in the same unit (row, column, or 3×3 box) contain, between them, only three different candidates.

  1. Each of the three cells can only take one of those three digits.
  2. Three cells, three digits: all three digits must be used up by exactly these cells.
  3. The cells do not need all three candidates each. {1, 3}, {1, 2, 3} and {2, 3} still form a valid triple.
  4. Therefore, those three digits cannot appear anywhere else in that unit!

Worked Example: Naked Triple in Row 1 Eliminates 2 from Cell R1C8

Look at the empty cells of Row 1 on the board below:

  • The three locked cells: R1C2 holds {1, 3}, R1C4 holds {1, 2, 3}, and R1C9 holds {2, 3}. Together they use only the digits 1, 2 and 3.
  • No smaller lock: No two of these cells share just two digits, so this is a true triple, not a hidden Naked Pair.
  • The Triple Lock: Row 1 needs a 1, a 2 and a 3, and these three cells can hold nothing else. So 1, 2 and 3 are claimed by R1C2, R1C4 and R1C9.
  • Target Elimination at R1C8: Cell Row 1, Column 8 (R1C8) has candidates {2, 8}. Because the triple claims Row 1's 2, candidate 2 is eliminated from R1C8, leaving 8 as a Naked Single!
  • Bonus elimination: R1C3 {3, 7, 8} also loses its 3.

⚠️ The Tempting Wrong Move: Placing 2 in R1C8

R1C8 has just two candidates {2, 8}, so a quick guess of 2 looks harmless.

Why this fails: Placing 2 in R1C8 removes 2 from R1C4 and R1C9. R1C9 is left with only 3, so R1C2 must take 1. Now R1C4 needs a 1 or a 3, and both are already used in Row 1. The cell has no legal digit, an immediate contradiction!

💡 Solving Tip: Count Cells, Not Candidates

When a unit has many two- and three-candidate cells, pick any three of them and write the union of their candidates. If the union has exactly three digits, you have a Naked Triple. Don't wait for three identical {a, b, c} cells: mixed pairs and triples are far more common. In the Ninely engine, a Naked Triple is an Expert-tier technique.
