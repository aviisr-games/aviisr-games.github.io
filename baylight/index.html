What makes Baylight different

Many sorting puzzles reward speed or punish a single mistake. Baylight was designed to feel like tidying a loading dock at the end of a shift: slow, satisfying and forgiving. There are no timers and no lives, so you can stop mid-level, think, and come back without losing anything.

The rule is simple. Parcels arrive one after another, you tap a bay to place the next one, and three matching parcels fill a truck that drives away. The challenge comes from limited space. Every parcel you place changes what the next few moves can do, so the game rewards looking ahead rather than reacting.

A few habits make levels easier. Look at the queue of upcoming parcels before you tap, and decide where each of the next two or three will go. Keep one bay for the color you see most. Use the pallet to park a parcel only when it buys you a better move, since it is a limited resource and a bay that jams is harder to recover than one you left open.

Accessibility shaped the design from the start. Each color carries its own pattern, so players with color blindness can tell parcels apart by shape alone, and nothing in a level depends on reaction speed. Rounds are short, and the game works offline, which makes it a good fit for a commute or a waiting room.
