Privacy Policy

Ninely for Android and iOS · Effective October 17, 2026 (updated November 26, 2026)

This policy explains what information the Ninely app ("the game", "we") uses and why. Questions: aviisr@aviisrgames.com

Information the game keeps on your device

There are no accounts. Your settings, language and number style, statistics, the puzzle in progress, your hint balance and what you bought are stored on your device. The game also keeps a short technical log on your device. Deleting the game deletes them.

Support messages

If you contact us (by email or from Settings > Send feedback), we receive your email address, your message and anything you choose to include. If "Include technical log" is on, the recent technical log (game version, device model, operating system, errors and game events, and the random game player ID used for leaderboards; no name or contact details) is attached to that email as a text file, and you see it before you send. The email itself also lists the game version, device model, language and that player ID. Emails are kept in our Gmail mailbox (Google). We use this only to answer you and fix problems, and delete it when it is no longer needed or when you ask. Crash and install statistics come from Apple and Google (App Store Connect, Google Play Console).

Game services

For leaderboards, game settings and understanding how the game is played, the game uses Unity Gaming Services, provided by Unity Technologies and its affiliates:

  • Anonymous player ID: created automatically. No account, no name, no email. It is shown in Settings.
  • Leaderboards: your best time per difficulty, under an automatically generated player name.
  • Remote settings: the game downloads settings (for example how often ads may appear) and which test group you are in.
  • Analytics: game events (puzzle started, completed or lost, hints used, ads shown and their estimated revenue, store offers shown, purchases) with device and app information (model, operating system, language, app version, country derived from your IP address). In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, analytics runs only if you agree. Elsewhere it is on by default. You can turn it off at any time in Settings > Share anonymous usage statistics.

Advertising

From version 1.2.8 the ads are served through Google AdMob mediation (the Google Mobile Ads SDK), which works with these ad networks: Google AdMob, Unity Ads, Meta Audience Network, Liftoff Monetize (formerly Vungle) and Moloco Inc. (Moloco Ads), an advertising network that may receive your advertising identifier, IP address and ad interaction data to deliver and measure ads. OpenX may also bid for ad space through Google's Open Bidding inside AdMob. Versions 1.2.4 to 1.2.7 also worked with InMobi and Chartboost. Versions before 1.2.4 use Unity LevelPlay (ironSource), which works with Google AdMob, Unity Ads, Meta Audience Network, Chartboost, InMobi and Liftoff Monetize, and monitor ad quality with Unity Ad Quality, until you update the game. To show and measure ads, limit how often you see the same ad and prevent fraud, these services may collect: your device's advertising identifier (on iPhone and iPad only if you allow tracking), an app-specific identifier, IP address (and approximate location derived from it), device and app information, and how you interact with ads. Each network's use of this data is governed by its own privacy policy:

  • Google AdMob: policies.google.com/privacy
  • Unity Ads: unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy
  • Meta Audience Network: facebook.com/privacy/policy
  • Liftoff Monetize (Vungle): liftoff.ai/privacy-policy
  • InMobi (up to version 1.2.7): inmobi.com/privacy-policy
  • Chartboost (up to version 1.2.7): docs.chartboost.com/en/legal/privacy-policy
  • Moloco Inc. (Moloco Ads): moloco.com/terms-and-policies/privacy-portal

These ad networks' SDKs may also collect crash and performance diagnostics about their own code, under their own privacy policies linked above. On Android, where the law requires it, the game reads the age signal that Google Play provides and uses it only to show age-appropriate ads.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, ads are personalized only if you agree: the game asks for consent with Google's consent message (User Messaging Platform), where you can also choose which ad partners may use your data, and you can change your choices in Settings > Privacy choices. Elsewhere ads are personalized by default, and you can turn that off in Settings > Personalized ads. On iPhone and iPad, tracking also requires your permission in the system prompt. You see the same number of ads either way. Where US state privacy laws apply, a request not to sell or share your personal information is passed on to each network.

Purchases

Optional purchases such as Ninely Pro and hint packs are processed by the Apple App Store or Google Play. We never receive your payment details. The game receives a purchase confirmation so it can unlock what you bought. Restore purchases is in Settings.

Children

Ninely is a general-audience puzzle game and is not directed to children under 13 (16 in the EEA). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Your choices and rights

You can turn off personalized ads and analytics in Settings, reset your advertising identifier in your device settings, and delete all data on your device by deleting the game. To ask for access to, or deletion of, data that Unity services hold for your anonymous player ID, email aviisr@aviisrgames.com with the player ID shown in Settings. People in the EU and UK, California, Israel and Saudi Arabia have rights under their local privacy laws; we honor them by email request.

Changes

If the game's use of data changes, we will update this page and its effective date.
