2030. Can you hold the region for one term?

Pick one of eight capitals, take the chair, and govern one month at a time through a term of six to eight months. Each month one decision is enough (a second is optional), then one tap prints the morning paper and tomorrow's front page shows what your choices did.

New players get a short guided first term that ends at their first paper, and every finished term builds a career: Rising Leader, Negotiator, Strategist, Veteran, Statesman.

About the game and how to play it well

Middle East 2030 is a work of fiction. It takes place in an invented 2030, and every event, headline and leader in it is made up for the game. It is a strategy simulation meant to be read and thought about, and its tone fits an Everyone 10+ rating: the consequences are shown through newspaper headlines and numbers, not through graphic scenes.

We chose a morning newspaper as the main screen because it makes cause and effect easy to see. You act in the evening, and the next front page tells you what happened. That loop teaches the central lesson of the game: a choice that helps one group can cost you trust with another, and the bill often arrives a month later.

Each term is short, six to eight months, so you can finish one in a sitting and try a different approach straight away. The eight capitals start with different pressures, which is why the same decision can work well in one chair and badly in another.

To do well, avoid chasing a single measure. Check every gauge before you commit, notice which problem is growing fastest and deal with it early, and keep the optional second decision for the month when you can afford it. Losing a term is useful, because the paper explains why. The 3-month briefing runs in your browser, and the chair guide describes each starting position.
