Middle East 2030 Coalition Strategy: Scenario Timeline & Chair Guide

In Middle East 2030, a full term lasts 6 to 10 months (8 to 12 minutes of play). Each month you sit in one of eight regional capitals, weigh two cabinet decisions (plus an optional third using political capital), and face the next morning's newspaper front page. Unlike sprawling spreadsheet wargames, every economic decision carries an immediate political price, and both a compromise and a strike can bring your government down.

1. Scenario Timeline: Dated Historical Baseline vs. In-Game Fiction

To anchor the simulation without blurring real events and invented mechanics, the game separates the dated historical record (through autumn 2026) from the fictional three-year bridge (2027 to 2029) that sets the board on 1 January 2030.

Dated Historical Context (2023 to Nov 2026)
  • October 2023 to 2024: Following the October 7 attack and the war in Gaza, regional conflict widened across the northern border, the Red Sea shipping lanes, and direct missile exchanges between Israel and Iran, alongside the December 2024 collapse of the Assad government in Damascus.
  • 2025 to 2026 (Dated Baseline as of November 2026): After the June 2025 twelve-day war, autumn 2025 Gaza ceasefire, and early-2026 regional escalations around Bab el-Mandeb and Hormuz, diplomatic tracks and ceasefire memoranda froze active fronts without resolving underlying strategic rivalries.
In-Game Fictional Extrapolation (2027 to 2030)
  • 2027 ("The Long Ceasefire", Fictional): Borders remain quiet on paper while state and non-state actors rebuild stockpiles with low-cost autonomous drones; high-energy laser air defense enters operational service and changes interception economics.
  • 2028 ("The Threshold Year", Fictional): Dispersed enrichment sites and competing intelligence estimates trigger cabinet splits across capitals over preemption versus containment.
  • 2029 to 1 January 2030 ("The Fall", Fictional): A budget freeze and grid crisis topple the outgoing cabinet. You take office on 1 January 2030 with a narrow mandate: 65% Coalition, 60% Economy, and 55% Stability.

2. Worked Policy Fork: Month 1 Cross-Border Energy Pipeline Accord

In Month 1 of the playable 3-Month Crisis Briefing, neighboring states propose an expedited joint natural gas and solar grid. Your coalition whip, Rafi Shoham, warns that hardliners will rebel against shared grid sovereignty, while Finance Minister Michal Avidar warns that rejecting the treaty will trigger industrial brownouts and credit downgrades.

Select either policy option below (using mouse, touch, or keyboard Tab / Enter) to inspect how the engine resolves the trade-off before Month 2 begins:

MORNING HEADLINE: "HISTORIC POWER GRID ACCORD SIGNED DESPITE PARLIAMENT ROW"
Coalition: 65% ➔ 60% (-5) Economy: 60% ➔ 72% (+12) Stability: 55% ➔ 63% (+8)

Strategic Consequence: Signing the accord cushions your Economy (+12) and regional Stability (+8) ahead of Month 2's currency run, allowing you to survive an interest-rate hike (`Path AAA` or `Path AAB`). However, dropping to 60% Coalition leaves little margin if you make two more anti-coalition concessions.

The Five Term Endings

Every 6-to-10-month run in Middle East 2030 concludes with a special edition newspaper front page recording one of five outcomes:

  1. Stay in Office: Complete your constitutional term with Coalition, Economy, and Stability above collapse thresholds.
  2. Removed from Office: Triggered by coalition collapse, cabinet revolt, sovereign default, or domestic unrest.
  3. Strategic Embargo: Triggered when international isolation or maritime escalation severs trade and financial clearing.
  4. Neighbor Reaches Threshold First: Triggered when regional proliferation clocks expire while your cabinet is paralyzed by internal crises.
  5. Quiet Month Holds: Achieved by threading the needle between a lopsided deal and a reckless strike until the term clock expires in calm.
