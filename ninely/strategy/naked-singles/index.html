How to Find Naked Singles in Sudoku

By Avi Israeli · Reviewed by Ninely Engine Logic Council · Updated November 2026

What is a Naked Single?

A Naked Single (often called a Sole Candidate) occurs when a single empty cell can only contain one possible digit because the other eight digits (1 through 9) are already present in its peers—that is, in its row, its column, or its 3×3 box.

Unlike a Hidden Single (where a number can only go in one spot in an entire house), a Naked Single is identified by looking directly at the cell itself and ticking off all digits blocked by its intersecting neighbors.

Worked Example: Cell Row 5, Column 5

Consider the central cell at Row 5, Column 5 (center of the board). Let us examine every intersecting unit:

  • Row 5 contains: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9
  • Column 5 contains: 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8
  • Center 3×3 Box contains: 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9

Now count through digits 1 to 9 for the target cell:

1 (blocked by row), 2 (blocked by box), 3 (blocked by row), 4 (blocked by col), 6 (blocked by box), 7 (blocked by col), 8 (blocked by row), 9 (blocked by box).

The only surviving digit is 5!

⚠️ The Tempting Wrong Move: Guessing 7 in R5C5

A player without complete pencil marks might see that 7 is missing in Row 5 and hurriedly place 7 in the center cell (R5C5).

Why this fails: Column 5 already has a 7 in Row 1! Placing 7 in R5C5 creates an illegal duplicate in Column 5. Later in the solve, Row 9 will have no legal placement for its 7. In Ninely, Smart Hints will alert you to the row and column collisions before you commit the error.

💡 Solving Tip: The "Rule of 8"

Whenever a row and column cross at a cell that shares a crowded 3×3 box, count the distinct numbers in all three units. If they sum to 8 distinct digits, you have found a Naked Single!
