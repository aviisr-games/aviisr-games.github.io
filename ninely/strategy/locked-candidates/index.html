Locked Candidates in Sudoku: Pointing Pairs & Box-Line Reductions

By Avi Israeli · Reviewed by Ninely Engine Logic Council · Updated November 2026

What Are Locked Candidates?

When you advance from beginner to intermediate or expert Sudoku puzzles, singles alone are no longer enough. You need candidate eliminations to unlock the board.

Locked Candidates (also known as Intersections) happen when the possible spots for a number in one unit are constrained in a way that affects an intersecting unit. There are two complementary types:

  1. Type 1: Pointing Pair / Triple (Box to Line): Inside a 3×3 box, all candidates for a digit lie strictly along a single row or column. Therefore, that digit must be placed on that line inside this box—and cannot appear anywhere else in that row or column outside the box!
  2. Type 2: Box-Line Reduction (Line to Box): Along a row or column, all candidates for a digit lie strictly within a single 3×3 box. Therefore, that digit cannot appear in any other cell of that 3×3 box!

Worked Example: Pointing Pair in Box 2

Look at Box 2 (top middle, Rows 1–3, Columns 4–6). Suppose digit 7 can only appear in Row 2, Column 4 and Row 2, Column 5.

  • Because 7 must go into one of these two cells to satisfy Box 2, 7 is locked into Row 2.
  • Now trace Row 2 across into neighboring Box 3 (top right). Any pencil mark for 7 in Box 3 along Row 2 (e.g. at R2C8 or R2C9) is eliminated!
  • Removing 7 from R2C8 instantly turns R2C8 into a Naked Single (e.g. placing 3)!

⚠️ The Tempting Wrong Move: Placing 7 in R2C8

A player who notices that Row 2 needs a 7 might place it casually in R2C8 without checking Box 2.

Why this fails: Placing 7 in R2C8 means that Row 2 inside Box 2 cannot contain 7. But in Box 2, Row 2 was the only line with candidate 7 remaining! Now Box 2 has zero valid cells for 7, immediately corrupting the game. In Ninely, Smart Hints reveal this locked alignment and explain the elimination step before you get trapped.

💡 Solving Tip: The Box-to-Line Scan

When you finish placing simple singles and hit your first roadblock, scan each 3×3 box one by one. Check if the remaining candidates for any number line up in the same horizontal row or vertical column. If they do, project that line into neighboring boxes and cross off candidates!
